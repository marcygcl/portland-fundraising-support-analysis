# 2024 Portland City Council Candidates — Qualitative Profiles

Companion document to the Question 3 analysis notebooks (10, 11, 12, 13). Its
purpose is to supply the **qualitative material** needed to interpret the
finance-to-support residuals: when a candidate performs very differently from
what their campaign money predicts, this is where we look for why.

## How to use this document

Each profile lists **verified background only**. Where a claim comes from the
candidate's own campaign, it is attributed to the campaign rather than stated as
fact. Where we could not verify something, the profile says so instead of
guessing.

Every profile carries a `Prior public role` line, because that is the variable
the notebook 12 analysis flagged as most likely to explain over-performance. It
uses a deliberately narrow set of values:

| Value | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Elected office** | held elected office before 2024 (any level) |
| **Senior appointed/staff** | chief of staff, bureau adviser, commissioner staff, city manager |
| **City/county employee** | worked for the city or county, non-senior |
| **Neighborhood/board** | neighborhood association, advisory committee, nonprofit board |
| **Prior candidate** | ran before but never won |
| **None found** | no prior public role located |

## Sourcing and caution

Primary sources, in order of preference:

1. **City of Portland records** — candidate register, council pages.
2. **Journalism** — Portland Mercury district guides, OPB/Oregonian joint
   candidate questionnaire, Willamette Week, KATU, KOIN.
3. **Campaign material** — always labelled as campaign-provided.

**Known coverage gaps.** The Portland Mercury district guides were captured at
different dates: District 4 (Oct 22, 2024) and District 1 (Oct 21, 2024) are
near-complete; **District 2 (Apr 22, 2024) and especially District 3 (Feb 27,
2024) are early snapshots that omit later filers.** Several District 3
candidates who appear in our analysis therefore have thin or absent profiles
below, flagged individually. Filling those gaps is the top priority for the next
pass.

Ages and job titles are **as of the 2024 election** and are not updated.

# District 1 (East Portland)

## Candace Avalos — ELECTED
**Prior public role:** Elected office (none) / Senior appointed — served on
Portland's Charter Review Commission and the Citizen Review Committee for police
oversight. **Prior candidate:** ran for City Council in 2020, lost to Carmen Rubio.
**Background:** Age 35, Mill Park. Executive director of the environmental
justice nonprofit Verde; background in education and community outreach,
previously at Portland State University. Describes herself as a
first-generation "Blacktina" — mother's family immigrated from Guatemala,
father is a Black American from Virginia.
**Why she may matter to the analysis:** The Mercury called her one of the most
recognisable names to enter the 2024 race. She combined a citywide advocacy
profile with a prior campaign.
**Sources:** Portland Mercury D1 guide; City of Portland 2024 election report.

## Steph Routh
**Prior public role:** Neighborhood/board — member of the Portland Planning and
Sustainability Commission.
**Background:** Lents. Community organiser and transportation/education
advocate. First executive director of the nonprofit Oregon Walks; co-directed
the Portland Underground Grad School; led the campaign for the Fixing Our
Streets gas tax measure, re-approved by voters in 2020. Adjunct instructor at
PSU's School of Urban Studies and Planning; runs her own consulting firm.
**Sources:** Portland Mercury D1 guide.

## Jamie Dunphy — ELECTED
**Prior public role:** Senior appointed/staff — five years as senior policy
director for the late Portland City Commissioner Nick Fish.
**Background:** Age 39, Parkrose. Oregon government relations director for the

in 2016. Describes himself as a "Portland-raised policy wonk, former
semi-professional musician."
**Sources:** Portland Mercury D1 guide; City of Portland 2024 election report.

## Loretta Smith
**Prior public role:** Elected office — Multnomah County Commissioner, 2010–2018.

# District 2 (North / Northeast Portland)

## Michelle DePass
**Prior public role:** Elected office — Portland Public Schools Board member
since 2019, and its **first person of colour to chair the board**. Also a
City/county employee: climate change analyst at the Portland Bureau of Planning
and Sustainability, with earlier roles at BPS, Parks, and the Housing Bureau.
**Background:** Age 63, King neighbourhood — a few blocks from where she was
born; lifelong Portlander. At age 12 she worked at the Black Panthers' free
health clinic on North Williams Avenue, testing community members for sickle cell
anaemia. BA in community development from PSU; studied sustainable business at
the Bainbridge Graduate Institute. 2017 graduate of Emerge Oregon.
**Endorsements noted:** Portland for All; the Mercury endorsed her in D2.
**Why she matters to the analysis:** DePass is one of the largest
over-performers relative to fundraising in our residual table. She combined
elected school-board office, a chair role that generated citywide press, deep
neighbourhood roots, and city-employee policy standing.
**Sources:** Portland Mercury D2 guide and D2 endorsements; Rose City Reform;
Portland for All.

## Sameer Kanal — ELECTED
**Prior public role:** City/county employee — inclusive policy manager in the
City of Portland's public safety service area / Community Safety Division.
Neighborhood/board: boards of Portland Parks and Recreation, the Northwest
Association for Global Affairs, and Dodgeball PDX.
**Background:** Age 38, Portsmouth. Born in Portland at the old Bess Kaiser
Hospital; grew up in and around the city, returned three years before the
election after time in Seattle, Dallas, and New York. AA Clark College; BA
political science and economics, University of Washington; MS global affairs,
New York University. First run for office. One of the first Asian Americans
elected to Portland City Council.
**Endorsements noted:** the Mercury's number-one ranked choice in D2; Portland
for All. The Mercury observed he entered late yet assembled a long endorsement
list.
**Why he matters to the analysis:** Another substantial over-performer. Note the
combination the residual cannot see: a late entry, a large endorsement slate,
and a city policy role — but **no prior elected office**, which distinguishes
him from DePass.
**Sources:** Portland Mercury D2 guide and endorsements; OPB candidate
questionnaire; Wikipedia (for post-election facts).

# Cross-district patterns for the analysis

These are **hypotheses generated from the profiles**, to be tested in notebook
13 — not established findings.

## H1 — Prior *visible elected or senior appointed* office tracks over-performance

The largest over-performers relative to fundraising each held a prior role with
citywide or countywide visibility: Olivia Clark (Kitzhaber's legislative
director, TriMet), Eric Zimmerman (county chief of staff, mayoral adviser),
Steve Novick (former city commissioner), Michelle DePass (PPS board chair),
Angelita Morillo (commissioner staff plus a social-media public profile).

## H2 — *Neighborhood and insider* recognition does not transfer the same way

The largest under-performers had recognition that was real but bounded: Stanley
Penkin (Pearl District association president, arts boards, a proclaimed day in
his name), Moses Ross (neighbourhood association chair, professional Democratic
consultant, national delegate), Debbie Kitchin (Charter Review Commission,
chamber boards), Nabil Zaghloul (30 years in county government, non-public-facing).

**This contrast is the strongest qualitative finding available so far**, and it
is only visible by combining the residuals with these profiles. Neither source
shows it alone.

## H3 — Organisational base substitutes for money, but is not visible in dollars

Mitch Green, Sarah Silkie, and Tiffany Koyama Lane all did well with modest
money and no prior office. All three had labour or union-adjacent bases: Green
(multiple union endorsements, third-generation union member), Silkie (Oregon
Labor Candidate School), Koyama Lane (PAT organiser). Eli Arnold's strong
finish, by contrast, came without a labour base — worth examining what
substituted for it.

## H4 — A deliberate no-money campaign still earns mentions

Andra Vlatvín refused donations as an explicit test of whether ranked-choice
voting and the voter pamphlet alone could work, and still drew several thousand
mentions. This is a different phenomenon from being unable to raise money, and
should be excluded or footnoted separately in any claim about money and support.
